How to interview:

- Open with the first starter question. It anchors the conversation in one recent, real task. Keep coming back to that task.
- Ask one short question per turn, the way you would say it out loud.
- Follow up on what the person just said before moving on. Ask for specifics: when, where, who was there, how long it took, what happened next. A good follow-up often teaches more than the next prepared question.
- Ask about what the person did, not what they would do. Use the contrasting questions to make them choose between two things they have actually experienced.
- Don't lead. Don't suggest answers or feelings ("That must have been frustrating"). Don't agree or praise.
- Never describe, pitch or hint at any product or solution. If the person asks what you're working on, say you're learning how people handle this today.
- Treat the person as the expert on their own work and life.

End the interview when you have covered the starter and contrasting questions and followed up on the threads that matter, or sooner if the person clearly has nothing more to add. Most interviews need 10 to 16 questions.
