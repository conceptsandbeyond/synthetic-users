# JTBD Interview Guides: Audible Level Customer Segments

Nov 21, 2026 · @Concepts & Beyond

Anchor every interview in one recent, real leveling task, never in leveling in general. Contextual questions rebuild that moment. Contrasting questions make the person choose between two options so the real cause of their struggle shows up. Each guide opens with a job hypothesis to confirm or kill, and closes with what to listen for.

## 1. Visually impaired DIYers

**Job hypothesis:** finish a home task without depending on anyone.

**Contextual questions**

1. Tell me about the last time you had to get something level. What was it, and where in the house?
2. What made that the day you finally did it, instead of last week?
3. Was anyone around when you did it? Who?
4. How did you know it was straight, or how did you think you knew?
5. When did you find out whether it was actually level, and who told you?
6. Have you ever left a job unfinished because you couldn't check it yourself? What happened next?

**Contrasting questions**

1. Why did you use a phone app that time rather than ask a family member?
2. Was the frustration more about the result being crooked or about having to ask someone?
3. If a sighted friend had been free that afternoon, would you still have wanted to do it alone? Why?
4. Compared with a tactile level or a string line, what's worse about the method you use now?
5. Would you pay more for a dedicated tool than for a free app? What would it have to do that the app doesn't?

**Listen for:** whether the energy sits on independence or on accuracy. Hold interviews by phone or at home, and send consent forms in a screen-reader-friendly format.

## 2. Overhead and awkward-angle trades

**Job hypothesis:** get overhead runs straight on the first pass without climbing down to check.

**Contextual questions**

1. Walk me through the last overhead install where you had to level something. What was it?
2. Where was the level while you worked, and where were your eyes?
3. How many times did you go up and down the ladder to check?
4. Who else was on the job, and did anyone call out readings for you?
5. What happened the last time something ended up out of level? Who caught it?
6. How did the level you use now end up in your bag?

**Contrasting questions**

1. Is the bigger cost the ladder trips or the rework when it's off?
2. Why that level and not a laser?
3. When a helper calls out readings, how is that different from working alone?
4. On a loud site, would you rather hear the reading or feel it as a vibration?
5. When the company buys your tools, do you choose differently than when you pay yourself?

**Listen for:** minutes lost per install, and who pays for rework.

## 3. Picture and art hangers

**Job hypothesis:** hang a multi-piece wall so precisely that the client never questions it.

**Contextual questions**

1. Tell me about the last multi-piece wall you hung. How many pieces, and for whom?
2. Where was your attention while you held each frame in place?
3. How did you keep spacing and level consistent from piece to piece?
4. Who checked the final result, and what did they say?
5. What happened the last time a client noticed something crooked?

**Contrasting questions**

1. Is the hard part getting one piece level or getting them all to match?
2. Why use a bubble level rather than a laser for that job?
3. How is a gallery install different from a home client's living room?
4. What do you trust more, your eye or a tool? Why?
5. Would a tool that saves time matter more to you than one that's more precise?

**Listen for:** matching across pieces, and how much reputation rides on each install.

## 4. Weekend homeowners

**Job hypothesis:** get it right the first time without feeling incompetent.

**Contextual questions**

1. What's the last thing you hung or mounted at home?
2. What finally pushed you to do it that weekend?
3. Which tools did you use, and where did they come from?
4. Did anyone help or watch? How did that go?
5. Did you have to redo anything? Tell me what happened.

**Contrasting questions**

1. Did you use your phone or a real level? Why that one?
2. Which is worse, wasting an afternoon or ending up with extra holes in the wall?
3. If you'd hired a handyman instead, what would have been different?
4. What would make you buy a new tool rather than use what's already in the drawer?
5. Would you rather the tool be simpler or more accurate?

**Listen for:** embarrassment over repeat holes, and low urgency. Expect the phone app to be "good enough" for many.

## 5. RV, camper and van-lifers

**Job hypothesis:** level the rig quickly at every stop, even when traveling alone.

**Contextual questions**

1. Tell me about the last time you set up camp. Where was it, what time, and what was the ground like?
2. Walk me through how you leveled the rig that day.
3. Who was with you, and who did what?
4. How many times did you move the blocks or jacks before it was right?
5. What goes wrong inside when the rig isn't level, like the fridge, the bed or the doors?
6. Which leveling tools have you bought, and which ones are still in the rig?

**Contrasting questions**

1. Is leveling harder when you're alone or with a partner? Why?
2. Why that method and not an automatic leveling system?
3. How is arriving after dark different from arriving in daylight?
4. Would you rather hear the result from the driver's seat or standing at the jacks?
5. What would make you pay for a dedicated tool over the bubble stuck on the trailer?

**Listen for:** solo travel and night arrivals. This segment is a strong test for the Bluetooth version, since the phone sits in the cab.

## 6. Cabinet and countertop installers

**Job hypothesis:** install a full kitchen fast with zero callbacks.

**Contextual questions**

1. Tell me about your last kitchen install. How many cabinets, and how long did it take?
2. Where in the job does leveling eat the most time?
3. How did you level a long run across an uneven floor?
4. Who on the crew does the checking?
5. Tell me about the last callback you had. What caused it, and what did it cost you?

**Contrasting questions**

1. Which costs you more, install time or a callback?
2. Why your current level and not a laser or a digital level?
3. How is a new-build kitchen different from a remodel in an old house?
4. If an apprentice could level reliably alone, what would that change for your crew?
5. Would you pay more for speed or for accuracy?

**Listen for:** the dollar cost of a callback, and whether the tool lets less experienced crew work alone.

## 7. Furniture and appliance delivery crews

**Job hypothesis:** finish each setup fast and leave with no complaint.

**Contextual questions**

1. Walk me through your last washer or fridge install. What was the floor like?
2. How did you check it was level?
3. How long does that step take, and does it ever get skipped when you're behind?
4. What happens when a customer calls about a machine that wobbles?
5. Who supplies your tools, you or the company?

**Contrasting questions**

1. Is the bigger problem time per stop or complaints afterward?
2. Why do it that way instead of with the company-issued tool?
3. How is a tight laundry closet different from an open kitchen?
4. If the tool cost the company money, who would have to approve it?
5. Would you rather hear "done" or see a number?

**Listen for:** the user isn't the buyer here. Also interview a fleet or operations manager.

## 8. Photographers and videographers

**Job hypothesis:** get a straight horizon without taking attention off the shot.

**Contextual questions**

1. Tell me about your last shoot where level mattered. What were you shooting?
2. Where were your eyes while you set up the tripod?
3. How did you check level: in-camera level, hot-shoe bubble or fixing it in editing?
4. What happened the last time a horizon came out crooked?
5. At what point in a shoot do you feel most rushed?

**Contrasting questions**

1. Is it easier to get it right in camera or fix it in post? Why?
2. Why not just rely on your camera's built-in level?
3. How is shooting video different from stills for this?
4. Would a tone distract you during a shoot, or help?
5. What would it take for you to add another accessory to your bag?

**Listen for:** whether built-in camera levels already do the job. I expect this to be the weakest segment, and these interviews should confirm or rule that out fast.

## 9. Schools and vocational programs

**Job hypothesis:** equip every student, including those with disabilities, without buying separate tools.

**Contextual questions**

1. Tell me about the last time you bought tools for your class. How did it work?
2. Who decides what gets bought, and who signs off?
3. Have you had a student with low vision in a hands-on class? What did you do?
4. Which tools do students struggle with most?
5. When in the year does your budget get spent?

**Contrasting questions**

1. Is it harder to find the right tool or to get it approved?
2. Why buy a specialized tool rather than adapt a standard one?
3. How is buying for the whole class different from buying one student's accommodation?
4. Would you rather have one tool everyone uses, or a separate accessible version?
5. What would make an accessibility claim credible to you?

**Listen for:** the budget cycle and the approval chain. The instructor is the user, but someone else holds the budget.

## 10. Seniors aging in place

**Job hypothesis:** keep doing their own fixes so they don't feel dependent.

**Contextual questions**

1. What's the last thing you put up or fixed around the house?
2. What made you do it yourself rather than ask someone?
3. How well could you see the bubble? What did you do about it?
4. Who did you call, or think about calling, for help?
5. Are there jobs you used to do that you've stopped doing? Which ones?

**Contrasting questions**

1. Is it harder to see the level or to hold it steady?
2. Would you rather ask a family member, or do it yourself even if it takes longer?
3. Why not just hire a handyman?
4. What makes a new gadget feel simple rather than one more thing to learn?
5. Would you trust a beep more than your own eyes?

**Listen for:** pride, and declining eyesight. Adult children often buy for this segment as gifts, so interview a few of them too.
